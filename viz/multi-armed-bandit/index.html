Multi-armed Bandit

Choose the next trial among variants with uncertain success rates. Enter comparable success/failure evidence, compare what Thompson Sampling and UCB1 recommend, record each resolved outcome, and use the seeded simulation to see how the methods explore.

The interactive tool needs JavaScript. Without it, here is how the calculations work.

For a variant with s successes and f failures, and one shared prior Beta(a, b):

posterior = Beta(a + s, b + f) posterior mean = (a + s) / (a + b + s + f) 95% credible interval = posterior quantiles at 0.025 and 0.975 Thompson score = one draw from the posterior; recommend the largest UCB1 score = s/n + √(2 ln T / n), n = s + f, T = trials across all variants

UCB1 recommends the first untried variant before comparing scores, so ln 0 never occurs. Ties go to the first variant in display order. A UCB1 score is not a probability and is never capped at 1. Intervals are computed numerically from the regularised incomplete beta function, not by a normal approximation.

Worked Beta update. 8 successes in 100 trials (92 failures) with the prior Beta(1, 1) gives the posterior Beta(9, 93), whose mean is 9/102 ≈ 8.82%.

Assumptions, references and next steps

  • Each variant has a stable success probability while you experiment.
  • Trials are comparable: the same kind of unit, drawn from the same population and environment.
  • Every variant uses one common success definition, judged at a fixed horizon.
  • Every trial costs the same, so choosing one more trial of any variant is an equal decision.
  • Outcomes are recorded only once they are resolved; pending trials are not counted.

If the success definition, the audience or the environment changes, start a new experiment instead of adding to this one.

Next steps: follow either recommendation for one trial, record the outcome once it is resolved, and look again. Neither method declares an experiment finished or a variant conclusively best.

    Shared prior

    One Beta(a, b) prior is applied independently to every variant. Beta(1, 1) is uniform. Both parameters are numbers from 0.1 to 100.

    The page uses the xoshiro128** generator, seeded by splitmix32 and saved with your work, for Thompson draws; the simulation has its own streams. It is not cryptographic.

    Simulation

    The simulation reuses your variant names and the shared prior, but ignores your recorded evidence and never changes your experiment. You choose the true success probabilities; Thompson Sampling, UCB1 and equal allocation each get the same number of pulls and their own evidence. The kth pull of a variant receives that variant's kth reward under every method.

    Allocation per variant

    Pulls allocated to each variant, by method

    Expected regret adds up, over the pulls, the best known probability minus the known probability of the variant chosen. It uses the probabilities you set, not observed reward differences. Equal probabilities give zero expected regret; a probability of 0% always fails and 100% always succeeds. One seeded run does not establish that one method is better than another.
