Stealth aircraft: public RCS evidence

Assess public claims about the radar cross section (RCS) of the F-117, F-22, F-35 and B-2. Each claim shows its evidence, its result and the limits of that evidence.

Scope. This page does not rank the aircraft. It does not give an RCS value for a service aircraft.

A photograph or an outline does not give RCS. The only curves come from a NASA-funded test of an aluminum F-117 model, in dB with the reference not stated.

    F-117 Nighthawk

    F117-1: Shape and coating

    Claim
    The Air Force attributes reduced F-117 RCS to its faceted shape and coating.
    Grounds
    Its public account identifies both features.
    Warrant
    This source supports the attributed qualitative account of the design.
    Backing
    AFMC account, paragraph about shape and coating.
    Qualifier
    The support concerns the qualitative account. The source does not quantify individual effects.
    Rebuttal
    Photographs alone cannot verify those effects under radar test conditions.
    Result
    ✓ Supported within stated limits. Do not infer a numeric reduction or an aircraft ranking.
    Evidence type
    Official qualitative account
    Assessed scope
    The attribution in the Air Force account. Not the size of any effect.
    Conditions not stated
    No measurement. Frequency, aspect, polarization and test conditions are not stated.
    Source
    Air Force plans retirement ceremony to commemorate F-117, Derek Kaufman, 88th Air Base Wing Public Affairs, 2008-03-04. Paragraph 4, the paragraph about shape and coating. https://www.afmc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/154866/air-force-plans-retirement-ceremony-to-commemorate-f-117/

    F117-2: Model response

    Claim
    The NASA study compares measured and reconstructed responses from an F-117 model.
    Grounds
    The report identifies the model and separates the traces.
    Warrant
    This evidence supports a comparison within the model study.
    Backing
    NASA report, section 5.1 and figures 5.7–5.12.
    Qualifier
    The result concerns a model. Its magnitude does not establish absolute aircraft RCS.
    Rebuttal
    Transfer requires evidence for model fidelity and calibration.
    Result
    ✓ Supported within stated limits. Do not infer service-aircraft RCS.
    Evidence type
    Physical model measurement
    Assessed scope
    The comparison of original and reconstructed curves for one aluminum model in one report. Not the service aircraft.
    Conditions not stated
    Polarization, magnitude reference, calibration and experimental uncertainty are not stated.
    Source
    A Very Efficient RCS Data Compression and Reconstruction Technique (NASA-CR-191378-VOL-4), N.Y. Tseng and W.D. Burnside, The Ohio State University ElectroScience Laboratory, 1992-11. Section 5.1, printed page 76 (PDF page 86). Figures 5.7–5.12, printed pages 84–89 (PDF pages 94–99).. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930005618

    F-22 Raptor

    F22-1: Internal stores

    Claim
    GAO links internal F-22 stores to its low radar signature.
    Grounds
    The report describes internal stores and states that external stores increase RCS.
    Warrant
    The source supports a qualitative comparison of these configurations.
    Backing
    GAO/NSIAD-98-67, printed page 18.
    Qualifier
    The source does not quantify the benefit for stated radar conditions.
    Rebuttal
    The effect depends on the stores and test conditions.
    Result
    ✓ Supported within stated limits. Do not infer equivalence across store configurations.
    Evidence type
    Official qualitative account
    Assessed scope
    The qualitative statement about internal and external weapons in a 1998 report. Not a measured difference.
    Conditions not stated
    Radar conditions, the stores and the size of the effect are not stated.
    Source
    F-22 Aircraft: Progress in Achieving Engineering and Manufacturing Development Goals (GAO/NSIAD-98-67), U.S. General Accounting Office, 1998-03-10. Appendix I, Payload, printed page 18. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GAOREPORTS-NSIAD-98-67/pdf/GAOREPORTS-NSIAD-98-67.pdf

    F22-2: Full-scale tests

    Claim
    GAO documents full-scale F-22 pole tests in its 1998 report.
    Grounds
    The report describes tests with an F-22 on a pole.
    Warrant
    This evidence supports the existence of the reported programme.
    Backing
    GAO/NSIAD-98-67, printed pages 17–18.
    Qualifier
    The cited account does not disclose numeric results with complete conditions.
    Rebuttal
    A test programme can possess data that its public report does not release.
    Result
    ✓ Supported within stated limits. Do not infer numeric RCS or a current service configuration.
    Evidence type
    Official qualitative account
    Assessed scope
    The existence of the pole tests that GAO described in March 1998, during development. Not a current service configuration.
    Conditions not stated
    Results, frequency, aspect, polarization and configuration are not disclosed.
    Source
    F-22 Aircraft: Progress in Achieving Engineering and Manufacturing Development Goals (GAO/NSIAD-98-67), U.S. General Accounting Office, 1998-03-10. Appendix I, Radar Cross Section, printed pages 17–18. Also Low Observability, printed page 20.. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GAOREPORTS-NSIAD-98-67/pdf/GAOREPORTS-NSIAD-98-67.pdf

    F-35 Lightning II

    F35-1: Public design account

    Claim
    Lockheed Martin attributes F-35 stealth to aligned edges, embedded sensors, and internal weapons and fuel.
    Grounds
    The manufacturer identifies these features in its public description.
    Warrant
    The description supports attribution of this design account to the manufacturer.
    Backing
    F-35 design account, paragraph about stealth features.
    Qualifier
    This manufacturer account does not independently establish the contribution of each feature.
    Rebuttal
    A stated mechanism does not establish its measured magnitude on an aircraft.
    Result
    ✓ Supported within stated limits. Do not infer independent quantitative verification.
    Evidence type
    Manufacturer claim
    Assessed scope
    The attribution in the manufacturer account. The account also names reduced engine signature, which the claim leaves out.
    Conditions not stated
    The size of each contribution and any test conditions are not stated.
    Source
    F-35: The World's Most Advanced Fighter, Lockheed Martin, 2024-01-24. Section “More Than Meets the Eye”, paragraph 2. https://www.lockheedmartin.com/f35/news-and-features/f35-the-worlds-most-advanced-fighter.html

    F35-2: Signature after damage

    Claim
    The SigMA model retained its stealth signature after surface damage.
    Grounds
    Lockheed Martin reports baseline and damaged-model measurements and states that the signature remained intact.
    Warrant
    Independent verification requires the values and relevant test conditions.
    Backing
    F-35C test account, paragraphs about SigMA.
    Qualifier
    The account does not disclose numeric results or complete radar conditions.
    Rebuttal
    A valid development test can lack enough public evidence for independent verification.
    Result
    ? Cannot verify from public evidence. Do not infer a stated numeric RCS.
    Evidence type
    Manufacturer claim
    Assessed scope
    Performance after damage, as the 2008 release reports it. The page cannot check it.
    Conditions not stated
    Numeric results, frequency, aspect, polarization and the details of the damage are not disclosed.
    Source
    F-35C Stealth on the Carrier Deck Means High Performance, Low Maintenance, Lockheed Martin, 2008-03-18. Paragraph 5, the paragraph about SigMA. https://news.lockheedmartin.com/2008-03-18-F-35C-Stealth-on-the-Carrier-Deck-Means-High-Performance-Low-Maintenance

    B-2 Spirit

    B2-1: Shape and materials

    Claim
    The Air Force identifies the B-2's shape, composite materials, and coatings as contributors to low observability.
    Grounds
    Its fact sheet lists these features.
    Warrant
    The source supports the attributed qualitative account of the design.
    Backing
    B-2 fact sheet, paragraph about low-observability processes.
    Qualifier
    Low observability includes more than radar signature. The source does not quantify individual effects.
    Rebuttal
    The visible shape alone cannot establish the complete aircraft's RCS.
    Result
    ✓ Supported within stated limits. Do not infer a numeric RCS.
    Evidence type
    Official qualitative account
    Assessed scope
    The attribution in the Air Force fact sheet. Not the size of any effect.
    Conditions not stated
    The size of each effect and any radar conditions are not stated.
    Source
    B-2 Spirit (fact sheet), U.S. Air Force, 2026-05. Features, paragraph 3, the paragraph about low-observability processes. https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104482/b-2-spirit/

    B2-2: Scope of early tests

    Claim
    GAO states that the early B-2 tests did not represent operational conditions or long-term wear and maintenance.
    Grounds
    The report identifies these limits of the initial programme.
    Warrant
    These exclusions limit the conclusions that the tests support.
    Backing
    GAO/NSIAD-91-188, printed pages 4–6, especially page 5.
    Qualifier
    This assessment concerns the initial programme, not every later B-2 test.
    Rebuttal
    Later tests might address these factors. Assess their sources separately.
    Result
    ✓ Supported within stated limits. Do not infer the radar signature after operational wear.
    Evidence type
    Official qualitative account
    Assessed scope
    The early Block 2 flight tests of the first B-2 development aircraft, as GAO described them in April 1991.
    Conditions not stated
    Numeric results and test frequencies are not disclosed. This page does not assess later tests.
    Source
    B-2 Bomber: Early Radar Signature Tests (GAO/NSIAD-91-188), U.S. General Accounting Office, 1991-04-16. Results in Brief, printed page 5 (context on printed pages 4–6). https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-91-188.pdf
