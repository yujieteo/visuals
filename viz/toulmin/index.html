Toulmin in 60 seconds

Stephen Toulmin's model, from The Uses of Argument (1958), breaks an argument into six parts. You make a claim, offer grounds for it, and rely on a warrant that explains why the grounds count. A backing supports the warrant. A qualifier says how strong the claim is, and a rebuttal names the conditions under which it would fail.

Good arguments make all six parts visible, so a reader can see exactly where they agree or disagree. Work through each tab, then use the checklist.
