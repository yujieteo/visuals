Structural Distortion Explorer

Exaggerated, qualitative visualisation. Not to scale, no units.

What am I looking at?

A small square of skin, the amber unit patch, sits on a thin-walled structure. Loads distort the structure; the patch shows what that does to the skin under it. Everything is exaggerated and nothing has units: the shapes are qualitatively right, not calibrated.

The pale-blue wireframe is the undeformed ghost; the grey body is the deformed shape. The tube, box and I-beam are cantilevers clamped at the dark block and loaded at the free end; the panel is loaded in its own plane along its edges.

The four effects

  • Tension and compression analytic stretch or shorten the member uniformly; the Poisson effect analytic thins it in tension and swells it in compression.
  • Shear analytic slides one side of the patch past the other: a square becomes a rhombus, stretched along one diagonal and shortened along the other. Rotate the patch to 45° to see those diagonals directly. Transverse shear follows the thin-walled shear flow, strongest in the webs.
  • Torsion analytic twists each section about the shear centre (Saint-Venant), putting closed skins in shear. Open sections such as the I-beam also warp analytic: the flange tips slide along the span in opposite directions (Vlasov). Restraining warping at the clamp holds them there and stiffens the beam. The circular tube does not warp.
  • Bending analytic curves the member with plane sections staying plane (Euler-Bernoulli): one side stretches, the other shortens. In the box flanges shear lag assumed shape makes the strain peak next to the webs.

Buckling assumed shape

Thin plates in compression or shear stay flat up to a critical load, marked ▲ on the slider, and then wrinkle: diagonal waves along the tension diagonal in shear, square waves in compression. The panel skin, the box walls and the I-beam web can buckle; the tube stays smooth. Stringers and frames split the panel into smaller plates, which raises the threshold and shortens the waves. The wave shapes and the thresholds are assumed, not solved: thresholds keep the right order but are squeezed so each case buckles within the slider range.

The inset

The flattened patch, with rigid rotation removed and its shape change enlarged: dashed ghost, amber deformed outline, principal directions as red (stretching) and blue (shortening) arrows, and the shear angle γ as an arc. It shows the membrane strain of the wall; wrinkles appear in 3D only.

Controls

Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom, right-drag or two fingers to pan; with the view focused, the arrow keys pan and Shift with an arrow key orbits. Drag the patch, or tap the body to move it; the patch sliders do the same from the keyboard. Colour maps are scaled to the largest value shown, so they show pattern and sign, not size.
