Étale Fundamental Group

From loops to covers to Galois symmetry

Every computation here is a picture first: a curve, its punctures, a loop, a finite cover drawn sheet by sheet, one fibre, the permutation a loop induces on it, the finite group that results, and the inverse limit of all of them. The algebra sits underneath. The guide is Tamás Szamuely, Galois Groups and Fundamental Groups (Cambridge Studies in Advanced Mathematics 117).

One symmetry, three worlds

A field extension, a circle wrapped twice round itself, and the squaring map on the punctured plane. In each, two points lie over one, and going once round swaps them.

GK = Gal(Ks/K) = π₁ᵉᵗ(Spec K, K̄)   The fundamental group is the symmetry group of covering theory.

Not “Galois groups are homotopy groups”, but: Galois theory and fundamental-group theory are the same theory of finite sets with a group action. For suitable complex varieties, π₁ᵉᵗ(X) ≅ π₁top(X(ℂ))^, the profinite completion: ordinary π₁ → keep only its finite quotients → profinite completion → étale π₁. (Higher étale homotopy is beyond this page.)

    The fibre-functor machine

    A geometric point x̄ glows on the base. The cover floats above as sheets; dashed lines drop to x̄. Pulling out the points above it gives the finite set Fx̄(Y), and a loop at x̄ permutes it. Doing this for every finite étale cover at once, compatibly, is the fibre functor Fx̄ : FEt(X) → FinSet, and π₁ᵉᵗ(X, x̄) = Aut(Fx̄).

    Geometric and arithmetic π₁

    Over a non-closed field k there are two kinds of motion: a loop moving inside the geometric cover world over k̄, and Galois moving the coefficients. 1 → π₁(Xk̄) → π₁(X) → Gk → 1.

    Profinite completion: ℤ → ℤ̂

    Every finite connected cover of the cylinder is z ↦ zⁿ for some n, and they map to each other when the degrees divide. Click a quotient to see its cover.
