# Use the Monte Carlo Probability Workbench

Live at <https://teoyujie.org/visuals/monte-carlo-workbench/>. A model is a record of parameters, random variables with their laws, definitions, quantities to estimate (a probability, an expectation or a ratio of expectations), decision alternatives, an objective and constraints. The page holds 60 behaviour experiments and 180 workflows, three for each law, for censoring, for each copula, for each process and for the conditional models, 12 examples of custom law inputs, and an expert editor for a custom model. The constructed laws name a family: `mixture_poisson(w = [0.7, 0.3], lambda = [2, 9])`, `compound_geometric(freq = 12, p = 0.4)`, `truncated_negbin(r = 0.5, p = 0.2, lower = 2, upper = inf)`, `empirical(x, w)` and `kde(x, w, h)`; the names compose, as in `truncated_mixture_geometric`. A line `law Sev(a) pdf(x) = a*x^(-a - 1) on [1, inf] where a > 1 obs [1.3, 2.2]` defines a custom law; the page checks its normalisation, non-negativity, monotonicity, boundaries, parameter constraints, consistency and observations (each checked, failed or unverified), labels each sampler exact, approximate or unavailable, states its approximation controls and error sources, and does not run a law with a failed check. A line `censoring: right T by C` (or `left`) makes the model observe `T_obs` and `T_event` only. A run draws replicates in blocks of 1,024 from a Philox4x32-10 stream for each seed, stream name, replicate and variable, so the result does not depend on the number of workers. The reference value of a quantity comes from a closed form (also for the maximum, the minimum or the sum of i.i.d. draws where the law is known), from linearity of expectation, from the enumeration of a discrete support, or from adaptive quadrature over the quantile functions of at most 2 continuous variables or over the exact law of one name. The stable law's CDF is a numerical integral, so its references are numerical. Group 5: a copula variable `U ~ claytoncopula(theta = 2, d = 2)` is a vector of uniforms, and `N ~ poisson(lambda = 3, u = U[1])` draws a law by the inverse transform of a copula component (Sklar's theorem); a process variable `W ~ brownian(T = 1, steps = 64)` is the path at the grid times, with `W[1]` at t = 0 and `last(W)` at T, and `first`, `last` and `cumsum` read paths. The reference values of a box probability of a copula and of the path quantities X[k], last(X), mean(X), the call and put payoffs of a normal or lognormal value, the law of a count or a chain state and the extinction of a branching process are closed forms; the passage probability `max(W) >= b` of Brownian motion and geometric Brownian motion is a continuous-time reference, which differs from the grid estimate by the monitoring bias. Every result has a claim tag: theorem, numerical approximation or finite-run observation. To change the page, read [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md).

| See a heavy tail | `plot=tail`: the survival function on log–log axes, with the slope −α of a regularly varying tail |
| Read the extreme-value fit of the rainfall series | `model=gev-rainfall`, `panel=diagnostics` |

| Read the conditions of a process and its paths | `panel=assumptions` (stationarity, stability, explosion, boundary and discretisation at the parameters in force) and the figure "Sample paths, ensemble bands and first passages" |
| See the dependence of a copula | the scatter figure and its tail boxes, with Kendall's tau of the points and of the copula |
| Estimate with multilevel Monte Carlo | a model with the parameters `steps` and `coarsen`, such as `model=gbm-option-mlmc`; the panel "Multilevel Monte Carlo", `mlmc_eps`, then `get_multilevel` |
| Find candidate laws for a problem from what is known about it | the guided interview: the "Interview" tab, the state fields `iv`, `iv_off` and `iv_pick`, then `get_interview`; "Make the model record" applies the chosen candidate as the custom model |
| Estimate a small probability, a probability of ruin, or the systemic ruin of a catastrophe test | the rare-event lab: the "Rare events" tab (`nav=rare`), the fields `r_problem`, `r_law`, `r_copula`, `r_params`, `r_method`, `r_compare`, `r_failure`, `r_options`, `r_size` and `r_reps`, then `get_rare_run` |
| Run a Markov chain, a sequential sampler, a particle filter or randomised quasi-Monte Carlo, and read split R-hat, the ESS (a diagnostic only), the weight degeneracy and the error against the reference apart | the lab: the "Chains" tab (`nav=chains`), the fields `c_example`, `c_method`, `c_compare` and the settings, then `get_lab_run` |
| See why a method fails on a heavy tail | the rare-event examples marked "fails", and the theory panels `ldp`, `tilting`, `ruin`, `pk`, `taildep` and `sensitivity` |
| Compare alternatives with common random numbers or not | `streams` (`common` or `separate`); the decision report shows the variance ratio of each paired difference |

## Inputs

The view state is in the URL fragment: `model` (a catalogue id such as `binomial-overbooking`, or `custom`), `params` (settings such as `tickets=190; comp=500`), `method` (`independent`, `inverse`, `rejection`, `stratified`, `antithetic`, `control`, `euler`), `compare`, `streams` (`common` or `separate`), `strata` (K = 2^strata equal strata, 1 to 6), `stratify` (the stratified variable; empty for the focus variable), `size` (n = 2^size replicates for each alternative, 10 to 22), `plot` (`pmf`, `cdf`, `survival`, `quantile`, `tail`), `seed` (0 to 2^32 − 1) `failure` (`none`, `envelope`, `stream_reuse`, `table_cut`, `control_mean`) and `mlmc_eps` (the target root mean square error of a multilevel run). The model text of the editor has one line for each part, for example `X ~ binomial(n = tickets, p = pshow) {passengers}`, `D ~ normal(mu = 400, sigma = 80) {loaves}`, `prob any_bumped = bumped >= 1`, `control C = D` (the control variate, an affine function of variables with known means) and `law Die table(k) = [1, 2, 3] probs [0.2, 0.3, 0.5]` (a custom law; the clauses are `on [lo, hi]`, `where`, `obs`, `probs` and `grid`, and in a CF `i` is the imaginary unit). The guided interview adds `nav=interview`, `iv` (the answers and the optional numbers, such as `k=cnt;g=evt;m=2.4;c=5`: question ids, then `m` the mean, `sd` the standard deviation, `lim` the upper limit and `c` the threshold), `iv_off` (rule ids switched off, comma-separated, such as `x8,wk`) and `iv_pick` (a candidate id that replaces the top candidate). The rare-event lab adds `nav=rare`, `r_problem` (`sum`, `ruin` or `cat`), `r_law` (`exponential`, `weibull` with k < 1, `pareto2`), `r_copula` (the copula of the shares in the catastrophe test: `independent`, `gaussian`, `gumbel`, `clayton`), `r_params` (such as `n=20; b=50` or `alpha=1.8; M=60`), `r_method` and `r_compare` (`direct`, `tilting`, `splitting`, `subset`, `ais`, `ce`), `r_failure` (`none`, `light_family`, `small_spread`, `nominal_start`), `r_options` (method settings such as `levels=6; p0=0.1; rho=0.1; beta=0.1; iters=4; tilt=1.3; arrivals=1.2`), `r_size` (N = 2^r_size paths in each replication, 8 to 16), `r_reps` (R replications, 2 to 64) and the sweep fields `r_sweep`, `r_from`, `r_to` and `r_points`. The lab of group 8 adds `nav=chains`, `c_example` (such as `chain-two-modes`, `tank-level` or `asian-option`), `c_params` (such as `rho=0.995`), `c_method` and `c_compare` (`metropolis`, `gibbs`, `hmc`, `smc`, `particle`, `rqmc`, `independent`; only those of the example's family), `c_size` (2^c_size draws after the warm-up, particles or points), `c_runs` (R independent runs, 2 to 32), `c_step`, `c_leap`, `c_start` (`dispersed`, `one_point`), `c_resample` (`systematic`, `stratified`, `residual`, `multinomial`, `none`), `c_ess` (the resampling threshold τ), `c_schedule` (`adaptive`, `fixed`), `c_temps`, `c_moves`, `c_scramble` (`lms_shift`, `shift`, `none`), `c_path` (`standard`, `bridge`), `c_plot`, `c_coord` and `c_q`; it shares `seed`.

## Exports

- **Run record:** `<model>-run.json` (`monte-carlo-workbench/run`, version 1): the generator name and version, the stream scheme, the seed, the settings, the model record, the status and the results. Loading it replays the run and compares the estimates.
- **Model record:** `<model>-model.json` (`monte-carlo-workbench/model`, version 1), with its custom law lines in `laws`.
- **CSV:** `<model>-results.csv` (one row for each method, alternative and quantity, with the variance ratio of a variance-reduction method and the streams setting) and `<model>-trace.csv` (the estimate after each block).
- **Figures:** each plot as SVG or PNG, also the sample paths, the copula scatter and the multilevel levels.
- **Lab run record:** `lab-<example>-run.json` (`monte-carlo-workbench/lab-run`, version 1): the generator, the seed, the example, its record and settings, the status, the reference, the results and the three kinds of diagnostic. Loading it replays the run and compares the estimates. `lab-<example>-results.csv` holds one row for each method and quantity, then one for each run.
- **Rare-event run record:** `rare-<problem>-run.json` (`monte-carlo-workbench/rare-run`, version 1): the generator, the seed, the problem record, the method settings, the status, the reference and the results. Loading it replays the run and compares the estimates. `rare-<problem>-results.csv` holds one row for each method and quantity.
- **beamdswitch deck:** `monte-carlo-workbench-beamdswitch.md` (voice `bf_emma`).
- **Data:** [data.json](https://teoyujie.org/visuals/monte-carlo-workbench/data.json) (`raw.json` here): the catalogue, with four public-domain datasets, among them 128 annual maxima of daily rainfall at Fort Collins (NOAA GHCN-Daily).

## Worked example

`get_run` on the default view (`binomial-overbooking`, seed 2026, n = 65,536) gives, for "Sell 186", P(at least one passenger loses the seat) = 0.009094 from 596 hits, with the Wilson interval 0.008396 to 0.00985 and the reference 0.008732 from the enumeration of the binomial support. The decision picks "Sell 186": it meets the constraint and its paired interval separates it from "Sell 180".
