How long will this queue take?

This page estimates how long a queue will take from three things you can see: the people ahead of you, the counters open and how fast each person is served. It needs JavaScript for the interactive version; the rule it starts from is below.

wait ≈ (people ahead × minutes per person) ÷ counters

Worked example

8 people ahead, 2 counters, about 2 minutes per customer.
Rough estimate: (8 × 2) ÷ 2 ≈ 8 minutes.

Actual waits vary: some people take much longer than others, so treat it as “about 8 minutes, likely somewhere between 6 and 12”. With no counters open the queue is not moving at all. For a food order, compare the time you have waited with the wait you were told, rather than counting down.

How this works

The rough rule is wait ≈ (people ahead × minutes per person) ÷ counters. Counters serve in parallel, so two counters move a line about twice as fast as one.

Service time is how long one person takes at a counter. Fast, Typical and Slow mean about 1, 2 and 4 minutes per person; you can type your own.

Uncertainty. People are not all alike, so the page runs your queue 400 times with service times that vary around the pace (a gamma distribution with shape 3, so a typical person is within about 60% of the pace). “About” is the middle run; “likely” holds 8 runs in 10. The runs use a fixed seed, so the same inputs always give the same answer.

Throughput is how many people the counters finish per minute: counters ÷ minutes per person. Watching real completions under Observe pace measures it directly, which is usually better than guessing; after you are served, the page works backwards from your actual wait to the pace that would explain it.

Arrivals behind you do not change your wait in a first-come, first-served line: only the people ahead of you are served before you.

Shared or separate lines. A shared line feeding several counters averages out slow customers: the next free counter takes the next person. With a line per counter, you wait only for your own line, so its length and any slow customer in it matter much more.

Food orders are prepared out of sight, so the page does not pretend to model the kitchen. It compares the time you have waited with the range you expect (0.8 to 1.3 times the wait you pick). Complexity and busyness are what-ifs. Only when you count orders coming out does it estimate the time to yours, from that rate alone.

The estimate assumes first come, first served, counters of similar speed, no priority lane and no long interruptions. Everything is calculated on your device; nothing is sent anywhere.
