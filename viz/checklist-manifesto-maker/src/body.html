Checklist Manifesto Maker

Write a short checklist for each pause point in a task, review it, then run it one pause point at a time. Your checklists stay on this device: no account, no AI, no network.

A checklist supplements a procedure. This tool records what you report; it does not detect conditions, control equipment or decide whether a procedure is safe. The examples are not approved procedures.

How the checklist works

What must a person do or confirm at each pause point before the task can continue?

Five categories, kept distinct

Step Normal step
An action the person marks as done.
◆ Check Critical check
A check with an explicit result (passed, failed, unknown or not applicable) that controls progress. Unknown never counts as passed.
■ Stop Stop condition
A condition that interrupts normal progress when the person reports it.
▲ Escalate Escalation condition
A condition that needs help from a named person or role.
↺ Recovery Recovery step
An authored action after a failure or a stop, on a recovery route with restart checks and a restart destination.

Pause points and modes

A pause point is a moment in the process when the person stops and uses a short checklist. Each pause point has one mode, chosen by its author:

  • Read–Do: read each item, do the action, then record its result.
  • Do–Confirm: complete the work, then use the checklist to confirm the required items.

Neither mode marks anything automatically. A failed or unknown critical check, or a reported stop condition, blocks normal progress.

Design targets

About 5 to 9 items per pause point, and under a minute to check them, not counting the task itself. These are targets, not limits: nothing is hidden or cut to meet them, and only a trial with the people who will use the checklist shows the time. Completed boxes do not prove that the work is correct, so the tool gives no single score.

Reference: WHO Surgical Safety Checklist implementation manual, page 13, which asks for each checklist to be tested where it is used.
