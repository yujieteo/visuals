Δ-Complex Cohomology

Compute the cohomology of the sphere, the torus and the real projective plane from two oriented triangles each: geometry → oriented simplices → boundary maps → coboundary maps → kernels and images → cohomology. Point at any entry and the page traces it back to an oriented face.

Hover or tap any simplex, matrix entry or group. ⌘K / Ctrl K opens commands; 123 pick a space, ←→ step.

This page computes simplicial cohomology from three small Δ-complexes, each a square cut along its diagonal into two triangles U and L with edges a, b, c. The boundary matrices come from the face maps and the gluing; the coboundaries are their transposes, δᵏ = ∂ₖ₊₁ᵀ, and Hᵏ = ker δᵏ / im δᵏ⁻¹ by the Smith normal form.

Conventions and self-test
  • A 2-simplex [p₀, p₁, p₂] has boundary Σ (−1)ⁱ dᵢ, where dᵢ deletes vertex i. Each face is compared with the edge it is glued to: +1 along the edge's arrow, −1 against it. A simplex whose arrow runs against its vertex order (or is flipped) negates its generator.
  • Rows of ∂₂ are edges, columns are 2-simplices; rows of ∂₁ are vertices, columns are edges. δᵏ = ∂ₖ₊₁ᵀ because (δφ)(σ) = φ(∂σ).
  • Hᵏ = ker δᵏ / im δᵏ⁻¹ is computed by the Smith normal form over ℤ and row reduction over 𝔽ₚ — never from ranks alone, which would miss the torsion in H²(RP²; ℤ).
  • Cup products use Alexander–Whitney: (φ⌣ψ)[p₀, p₁, p₂] = φ[p₀, p₁]·ψ[p₁, p₂].
  • Superscripts throughout: these are cohomology groups Hᵏ, not homology groups Hₖ.
