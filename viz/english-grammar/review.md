## Sources used for references

- Chapter titles, authors and page ranges: the book's contents on Cambridge Core and the
  publisher's front-matter sample held by the Library of Congress (`meta.json`). All twenty
  chapter page ranges were rechecked on 1 October 2026 against the Cambridge Core book page,
  which lists every chapter with its range; all matched.
- Section numbers, titles and start pages: the contents list printed on each chapter's first
  page, as published on Cambridge Core (Chapters 2–20), and the front-matter sample (Chapter 1).
  Sections listed on later pages of those contents lists were not visible, so they are not
  cited: fused heads, genitives, supplementation and the Chapter 16 treatment of the passive
  cite the chapter only, and Chapter 19 sections after §5.1.5 are not listed.
- Chapters 9, 13, 17, 18, 19 and 20 cite only sections whose titles plainly cover the concept.
  Sections whose titles quote one of the book's own examples (for instance Chapter 13 §2.2
  and §3, or Chapter 18 §2.2.1–§2.2.3 and §4.1.1–§4.1.8) are not cited. The word-structure
  examples avoid the words those titles use as illustrations (for instance *lamps* and *geese*
  rather than the plurals named there), and no sentence from a title is used.
- Page numbers are section start pages from those lists. No other page numbers are given.
- The book's text could not be consulted, and no analysis has been checked against it. Analyses
  follow the book's framework as known to the author. Section references in the table below are
  section titles from the verified contents pages, not checks of the text.

## Analyses settled from memory (informational)

Every analysis below was settled from the author's knowledge of the framework and has not been
checked against the book's text. The explorer does not aim at total fidelity to the book, so this
list is a note for readers and future editors, not a publication blocker.

| Concept / example | Question | Analysis used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clause type, auxiliaries | How the book draws main-clause subject–auxiliary inversion (*Is Kim ready?*): flat clause or discontinuous VP | Not settled from available pages. No inverted clause is drawn; interrogatives are shown as subordinate clauses and inversion is described in text and in the technical notes. |
| Noun phrase structure | Whether a nominal (Nom) is drawn when the head noun has no dependents | Nom is stored when the head noun has a modifier or complement, otherwise the NP's head is the noun. Stated in the concept's technical note. |
| Determiners and determinatives | Single-word determiner as `Det: D` while determinatives in other functions are DPs | Kept: a bare determinative in determiner function, a DP when a determinative is a modifier (*big enough*, *much better*, *the three students*). Stated in the technical note. |
| *The three students passed.* | Numeral after *the*: determiner or modifier | Modifier within the nominal; *the* fills the determiner function. |
| Adjuncts | Attachment of adjuncts | Post-verbal and pre-verbal adjuncts are dependents in the VP; front adjuncts are dependents of the clause. No extra VP or clause layer is posited. |
| Passive by-phrase | Function label | Complement, construction "internalised complement (by-phrase)", following the book's term for the passive by-phrase (Ch. 16; section not on the verified contents page). |
| Extraposition | Position and label of the extraposed clause | "Extraposed subject", a dependent in the VP, with dummy *it* as subject (Ch. 16 §7.1, Ch. 4 §3.2.2). |
| *who won the prize* | Subject relatives: is *who* a prenucleus with a subject gap? | Treated as prenucleus plus subject gap, consistent with the other wh relatives. Subject *wh* interrogatives (*Who called?*), where the same question arises, are not used. |
| *which*, *what* | Category of standalone *which* / *what* | Determinative (cited at Ch. 5 §7.13–7.14). Standalone, *which* and *what* are fused determiner-heads, and fused-relative *what* is head fused with prenucleus (Ch. 12 §6). |
| *Kim, my neighbour, fixed the fence.* | Where a supplement attaches | A clause dependent labelled Supplement with an explicit anchor; the subject NP is just *Kim*. |
| Coordination | Category label for a coordination | "Coordination", with the construction field naming what is coordinated (e.g. coordination of NPs). The coordinator is a Marker within the coordinate it introduces. |
| *to* infinitivals | Where infinitival *to* attaches | Marker on the VP (Ch. 14 §1.4.2); *for* marks the clause when there is a subject. |
| Auxiliaries | Dependent-auxiliary or catenative analysis | The catenative analysis (Ch. 14 §4.2.2): the auxiliary is the predicator and takes a non-finite clause complement. |
| *The meeting is on Monday.* | Temporal PP after *be* | Predicative complement (cited at Ch. 4 §5.2 and Ch. 8 §6.1). |
| *Kim knows that Pat left.* | Content clause after *know*: object? | Complement, not object; the explanation says that "object" is reserved for NPs in this analysis. |
| Genitives as determiners | Term and location | Subject-determiner, cited at Ch. 5 §4 (The determiner function). The genitive case itself is treated later in Ch. 5, outside the verified page. |
| Avoided items | Category of *yesterday*, *everyone*, *there* (existential) | Not used in v1 because their category could not be confirmed. |
| Clause polarity (Ch. 9) | Tests for negative clauses | Reversed-polarity tags and *neither* / *so* continuations. *Kim is unhappy* is a positive clause (affixal negation). |
| *Pat has not replied.*, *I prefer not to know.* | Category and attachment of *not* | An AdvP: an adjunct in the auxiliary's VP for primary negation, and an adjunct of the non-finite VP (before the *to* marker) for secondary negation. |
| *Kim didn't call.*, *Don't move.* | Negative auxiliary forms | One word each, the negative form of *do*, with a bare infinitival clause complement (the catenative analysis already used for auxiliaries). |
| *Not many people came.* | Where *not* attaches | A modifier of *many* inside a DP in determiner function; the clause is negative. |
| *Kim deliberately didn't answer.* / *Kim didn't deliberately answer.* | Scope and position of the adjunct | Before the auxiliary: dependent in the auxiliary's VP (wide scope). After it: inside the complement clause (narrow scope). |
| *If you ever visit, call me.* | Category of conditional *if* | Preposition taking a clause complement, as for *before*. |
| Comparative complements (Ch. 13) | Category of *than* and *as* | Prepositions heading the comparative complement. |
| *Kim owns more books than Pat.*, *the same bike as Pat* | Attachment of a complement licensed by a dependent | Labelled Complement in the NP (not inside the nominal), with the construction "indirect: licensed by *more*" or "by *same*". The term indirect complement is used from memory. |
| *Kim is as tall as Pat.* | Category of the first *as* | Degree adverb, modifier in the AdjP; it licenses the *as*-phrase. |
| *Pat is more careful than Kim.*, *less often* | Category of analytic *more* / *less* | Determinative (comparative of *much* / *little*) in modifier function, as for *much better*. |
| *Kim is taller than Pat is.*, *longer than I expected* | Comparative clauses | Reduced clause with a gap for the missing degree element, linked to the comparative phrase of the main clause; *than Pat* is analysed as *than* + NP, without deciding whether it is a reduced clause. |
| *Kim is the younger of the two.* | Analysis of *the younger* and *of the two* | Fused modifier-head; *of the two* a complement in the NP; *the two* with *two* a fused modifier-head. |
| *The delay was more annoying than serious.* | Metalinguistic comparison | Analysed like other analytic comparatives; the construction label names it. |
| *same*, *different*, *like* | Categories | *Same* and *different* adjectives; *like* a preposition. |
| Deixis (Ch. 17) | Category of *here* and *there* | Prepositions without a complement; *here* a goal complement of *bring*, anaphoric *there* an adjunct of *works*. |
| Antecedent links | How anaphora is drawn | A link (`ante`) from the anaphor to an antecedent in the same example, only on the reading described; never a tree branch. *She locked the door before Kim left.* has no link because the reading is ruled out. |
| *Kim fixed the fence herself.* | Function of the emphatic reflexive | Adjunct (NP) in the VP, linked to its antecedent. |
| *a blue one* | Antecedent of pro-nominal *one* | The noun *bike* alone, not the NP or the nominal *red bike*. |
| *Kim thinks so.* | Category and function of anaphoric *so* | Adverb phrase, complement of *thinks*, standing for a clause from the context. |
| Word structure (Ch. 18–19) | How inflection and word-formation are drawn | A separate structure inside one word: the word at the top with a lexical category; bases (function Base, a lexical category or Splinter) and affixes (function Affix, category Prefix or Suffix) below; layered derivation as nested bases. Operations without a separable affix (vowel change, base plurals, irregular negatives, conversion, clipping, back-formation) are one base with the operation named. |
| *isn't* | Base of a negative auxiliary form | The positive form *is* plus the negative suffix *·n't*. |
| *passers-by* | Head of the compound for the plural | The plural suffix on *passer*; *by* a preposition base. |
| *well-known* | Compound or phrase before a noun | A compound adjective (adverb + adjective bases) joined by a hyphen; after *be*, *well known* is an AdjP. |
| *brunch* | Pieces of a blend | Two splinters (term used from memory), each recording the word it comes from. |
| Punctuation (Ch. 20) | How punctuation is modelled | Marks are not constituents. Each is attached to the start, the end, or a boundary between two parts of one constituent; delimiting pairs record their partner; terminals close the top-level unit. A closing supplement mark absorbed by the full stop is not written separately. |
| *Kim cooked; Pat washed up.* | Category for two main clauses joined by a semicolon | A coordination with no coordinator. |
| *Kim had one aim: to win.* | What the colon introduces | A supplement (a to-infinitival clause) anchored to *one aim*. |

## Automated checks

`python3 scripts/build_english_grammar.py --verify`, `python3 -m unittest tests/test_english_grammar.py`
and `node --test tests/english-grammar.test.mjs` cover IDs and links, token boundaries, ordinary
nesting, declared gaps, fusions and supplements, sentence/inspector/tree consistency, canonical
names versus aliases, chapter metadata, determinism, zero external requests, release counts,
the size budget, style-guide contrast pairs, and fragment, traversal and search logic.

The three structures added with Chapters 9–20 are validated on their own terms rather than as
constituent trees:

- Word structure: the pieces must spell the word exactly; the top is a lexical category with no
  function; parts are Base or Affix; affixes are prefixes or suffixes, in prefix–base–suffix
  order; a single-base part must name its operation; a declared spelling alternation (doubling,
  e-deletion, y-replacement) must actually produce the written piece.
- Punctuation marks: each names a real punctuation token of the right character, sits at the
  declared boundary of its constituent, and pairs (parentheses, quotation marks, delimiting
  commas) open and close the same constituent; terminals close the top level; once an example
  is punctuated, every punctuation token belongs to exactly one mark.
- Antecedent links: the target exists, is pronounced, and does not overlap the anaphor.

`node --test tests/english-grammar-beamdswitch.test.mjs` checks that every lesson, including
the new ones, exports a narrated deck that carries its marks, word structures and antecedents.
